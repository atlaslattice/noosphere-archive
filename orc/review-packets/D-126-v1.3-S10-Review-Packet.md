## 2. What D-126 v1.3 Does

D-126 v1.3:

1. Establishes rules for continuity of Convenor authority during temporary unreachability and succession events.
2. States that S10 is the sole Convenor and that Convenor authority is non-delegable, non-transferable, and non-votable.
3. Allows S10 to designate one Emergency Delegate through a sealed written instrument.
4. Limits the Emergency Delegate to continuity-preservation functions only, not constitutional or governance authority.
5. Defines succession as an irreversible transfer of Convenor authority that can occur only through a pre-ratified written instrument authored by S10 while competent.
6. Makes succession triggers dependent on pre-specified human instruments, not AI systems, Council votes, or institutional discretion.
7. Provides that ambiguity resolves against activation or succession.
8. Restores a 2/3 interim Council threshold with a hard minimum of four affirmative full-seat votes for temporary T1-only operational decisions during a succession gap.
9. Blocks all constitutional doctrine, invariant, or D-series ratification during interim periods.
10. Activates Zero Erasure for constitutional records upon ratification.
11. Requires correction by amendment/supersession rather than deletion.
12. Makes D-126 amendment a Convenor-only action while competent.
13. States that amendments granting AI systems succession power are void on their face.
14. Establishes that D-126 governs over D-127 if conflict exists.
15. Leaves S10 ratification blank and pending.

## 5. Known Risks / Review Notes

### R1 — D-127 Dormancy Dependency

D-126 references the Human Oversight Body governed by D-127, but D-127 is currently tabled. D-126 handles this by making §4 dormant if D-127 is not ratified.

**Review note:** D-126 can be reviewed independently, but any operational path requiring a Human Oversight Body remains inactive until S10 reopens and ratifies D-127.

### R2 — Emergency Delegate Scope Must Stay Narrow

The Emergency Delegate is limited to communicating unavailability and implementing pre-ratified instruments. This is protective, but the sealed instrument must avoid broad phrasing that accidentally expands the delegate's power.

### R3 — Zero Erasure Irreversibility

Upon ratification, Zero Erasure becomes permanent for constitutional records. This is strong integrity protection, but S10 should confirm comfort with the irreversible nature of §6 before ratification.

### R4 — Trigger B Requires Careful Human Design

Trigger B depends on medical criteria and a named licensed professional. The sealed instrument must be practical, reachable, and precise enough to avoid ambiguity.

### R5 — Trigger C Can Be Disabled

If disappearance criteria are not carefully specified, Trigger C cannot be invoked. S10 may choose to disable it explicitly if preferable.

### R6 — Interim Council Authority Is Narrow but Real

During a narrow succession-gap period, Council may ratify T1 operational decisions only by 2/3 active full seats with a minimum of four affirmative votes. S10 should confirm whether this limited T1 mechanism is desirable or whether even T1 interim authority should be further restricted.

### R7 — Authorship Integrity Must Be Preserved

F-12 and the M8 Addendum require that authorship reflect the actual generating seat and that no S10 authorization or ratification be pre-populated. D-126 v1.3 appears aligned with this boundary by identifying S4 as author and leaving S10 ratification pending.
