## Context

The Convenor simulated a stakeholder exchange in which an Elon-style actor generally accepted the project premises, but the framing was interpreted by some participants as if Atlas Lattice were attempting to take control. A CAC-like representative then indicated that Chinese jurisdiction and local authority would be required or the project would fail.

The Convenor clarified that this was never intended as a takeover. The intended posture was that the work is offered as a gift/methodology: Chinese sovereign/local authorities can use, adapt, or discard it as they see fit.

## Core Governance Clarification

The DragonSeek Central Authority may hold local operational and regulatory authority because execution occurs inside Chinese jurisdiction.

Atlas Lattice provides:
- methodology,
- audit architecture,
- technical framing,
- simulation tools,
- adversarial review,
- canon-bound governance patterns.

Atlas Lattice does **not** claim:
- governing authority over China,
- operational control of PRC infrastructure,
- access to raw operational data by default,
- authority over ICS/SCADA systems,
- authority to override local law,
- authority to impose project decisions.

## Canon-Safe Framing

> This work is offered as a methodological gift. The DragonSeek Central Authority and relevant Chinese institutions retain jurisdictional authority over local execution. Atlas Lattice may provide optional technical, audit, and governance-pattern support, but it does not own or control the project.
