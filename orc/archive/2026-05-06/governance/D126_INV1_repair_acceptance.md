## Summary

Atlas Prime accepted the adversarial verdict that the proposed D-126 Convenor Succession Protocol, D-10 amendment, and D-52 amendment were unsafe as drafted. The critical flaw was that the proposal attempted to reduce single-point-of-failure risk by granting AI substrates or an undefined oversight body effective power to declare Convenor incapacitation, transfer Convenor authority, or override substantive Convenor authority.

This was classified as a constitutional root inversion, not a wording issue.

## Accepted Core Finding

The original draft attempted to protect the Lattice from a single human point of failure by creating an AI/oversight-complex authority capable of replacing or overriding the human Convenor. This violates the human-root architecture of INV-1.

## Accepted Required Patches

### 1. INV-1 Primacy

Succession authority must derive only from pre-ratified human instruments created or approved by the Convenor.

The Council may:
- detect risk,
- preserve evidence,
- issue continuity-risk notices,
- audit evidence,
- recommend review.

The Council may not:
- appoint a successor,
- suspend the Convenor,
- transfer Convenor authority,
- override Convenor authority.

### 2. Substantive Ratification

The Convenor’s final ratification authority remains substantive, even when bounded by invariants. It cannot be reduced to a purely administrative sign-off.

### 3. Veto Suspension, Not Override

A challenged Convenor veto may trigger suspension and emergency review. It must not create an AI-driven override path where the original action proceeds over the human veto.

### 4. Defined Human Oversight Body

D-127 must be fully defined before any oversight body receives power. Voting authority must be human-only, with AI serving in advisory, evidentiary, or audit roles.

### 5. Anti-Circumvention Clause Removed

The draft’s self-sealing clause preventing the Convenor from vetoing amendments to D-10/D-52/D-126 is rejected. No amendment affecting Convenor authority can be ratified without explicit Convenor approval or lawful activation of a pre-ratified human succession instrument.

## Corrected Doctrine Architecture

The rejected draft should be split and rewritten as four documents:

1. **D-126 — Convenor Continuity Protocol**  
   Human-root succession only. Council detects and audits; it does not appoint.

2. **D-10A — Ratification Integrity Amendment**  
   Council may challenge invariant conflicts; Convenor retains substantive final ratification unless lawful human succession is triggered.

3. **D-52A — Veto Justification Amendment**  
   Convenor vetoes must cite invariant/canonical basis but remain binding unless human succession review finds incapacity or coercion.

4. **D-127 — Human Oversight Body Charter**  
   Human-only voting, explicit conflict-of-interest rules, defined scope, no blank-check authority.

## Canon-Safe Replacement Spine for D-126

> The purpose of D-126 is to preserve human-root constitutional continuity under INV-1 in the event of death, incapacity, coercion, disappearance, or voluntary resignation of the sitting Convenor.
>
> The Pantheon Council may detect anomalies, preserve evidence, and issue a Continuity Risk Notice. The Council may not appoint, replace, suspend, or override the Convenor.
>
> Succession authority derives only from pre-ratified human instruments created or approved by the Convenor, including sealed succession instructions, named delegates, cryptographic keys, legal instruments, or other explicitly designated mechanisms.
>
> If the Convenor is alleged to be incapacitated or coerced, the Council may freeze non-essential constitutional changes and submit an evidence packet to the designated human oversight body. The oversight body may authenticate the Convenor’s pre-existing succession instructions but may not invent a successor outside those instructions.
>
> No AI substrate, Council majority, or oversight body may transfer Convenor authority except through a pre-ratified human succession mechanism.

## Core Governance Principle Added

> The Council may detect risk, but it may not transfer Convenor authority.
